## 4. Billing: what is done, and the one thing I did not touch

**Done and deployed:** the overage charge, the decline path, the plan upgrade, the
receipt, the plan-change notice, the page total, and an audit row on every one of them
so the next question like "did she get a receipt" is answerable from our own data
instead of from her inbox.

**NOT TOUCHED, on purpose.** `venueplay_founding.max_seats` is **1** for Jess while
`vp_venues.max_players` is **2** and Stripe is on **2**. `upliftPlan` updates the venue
and Stripe and never touches `max_seats`.

I did not fix it overnight. To do it safely I need to read every use of `max_seats`
across both Workers, and without Bash I cannot search a 330 KB file. Guessing at a
money column and shipping it unrun is exactly what put the receipt in the state it was
in last night.

**Instead I wrote something that measures it, and then I ran it.** Read-only, writes
nothing:

```
python3 venueplay-backend/tools/check-seats-agree.py
```

**It came back 7 accounts agreeing and 15 out of step**, which changes the answer:

```
enquiries@wellshothotel.com.au     seats 20   venues 0    stripe 20
indykingevents@gmail.com           seats 1    venues 2    stripe 2
jessgsmith11@gmail.com             seats 1    venues 2    stripe 2
```

`max_seats` is a **signup-time number that nothing maintains**. It is 1 on almost every
account regardless of what they actually have, and 20 on Wellshot because that is what
they signed up for. The uplift not writing it is not a special bug: nothing writes it,
ever, including the ordinary "add a venue" and "change your players" paths.

So this is smaller than it looked, and different in kind. It is not a money fault, it is
a dead column. The remaining question is only whether anything READS it for billing:

```
grep -rn "max_seats" venueplay-backend/worker/ venueplay/app/
```

* **nothing bills from it** -> leave it, or drop it, and this check stays as a report
* **something bills from it** -> then it is a real fault on 15 accounts and it jumps the
  queue

It prints, for every account, the three places that hold a player count: `max_seats`,
the sum of venue `max_players`, and Stripe's quantity. It deliberately does **not** fail
on a disagreement, because nobody has decided yet which of the three is meant to be
authoritative, and a check that fails before the rule exists is noise people learn to
ignore.

The answer it gives decides the job:

* **one or two accounts out of step** -> correct the rows, then make `upliftPlan` write
  `max_seats` too, and turn this into a gate check
* **most of them out of step** -> `max_seats` is not maintained by anything, and the
  question is whether it should exist at all

Run it and send me the table, plus:

```

```
