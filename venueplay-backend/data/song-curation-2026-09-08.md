# Song packs: what came out, what went in

8 September 2026. Written after the change was applied and both checks were run.

## Why this was done

Dean, on the packs: "I asked for the bangers that we would have heard in Australia, not
something we haven't heard of, for each category" and "I feel some of the songs in there
are awful."

He was right, and it is worth being precise about the cause. On 10 August 2026 a bulk load
put 12,880 "singalong tracks" into the library and poured whatever the search returned onto
the end of every playlist. Pub Classics finished on Buffalo Traffic Jam and Charley
Crockett. The Aussie pack finished on Earl Thomas Conley and The Wiggles. The host page
deals sixty songs a game weighted toward the front of the list, so most nights never reached
that far, but a long night, or a host who turns the hits weighting off, walks straight into
it. Songs nobody in the room has heard are dead squares: no singing, no shouting, nobody
looking up at the screen.

## How each pack was judged

A song stayed only if a mixed-age Australian pub or club crowd would place it inside the
first few seconds of a thirty second clip. Instinct was not enough on its own, so the
ranking and the filtering were done against evidence of what Australia actually bought and
voted for:

- ARIA and Kent Report year-end singles charts, every year from 1955 to 2026 (Wikipedia's
  "List of top 25 singles for YYYY in Australia" and the top 10 and top 100 pages).
- Australian number one singles, the 1950s through to 2026 (Wikipedia's "List of
  number-one singles in Australia during the ..." decade pages and the per-year pages).
- triple j Hottest 100, every countdown from 1989 to 2025, plus the Hottest 100 of All
  Time, of Australian Songs, of the 2010s and of the Past 20 Years.

That came to 12,500 chart placings. Each was matched to the library on a normalised title
and artist, and scored: a year-end number one or an Australian number one scores near 100, a
year-end placing scores by its rank, a Hottest 100 placing scores by half its rank. Where
the charts are thin, judgement filled the gap: the head of each pack was read song by song,
and enduring pub singalongs that never charted high here were kept on their merits. Khe Sanh
peaked at number 41 in Australia and is the most reliable singalong in the country.

Every pack now holds between 181 and 291 songs, ordered most known first. A game deals
sixty, so the smallest pack still has three times what a night needs.

## Ten examples of what came out, and why

1. **Hanging on Hope, Buffalo Traffic Jam** (was Pub Classics, 2026). A 2026 release from an
   act with no Australian chart history at all. It closed the flagship pack.
2. **Run Horse Run, Charley Crockett** (was Pub Classics and Country). American alt country,
   never charted here, and three of his tracks sat in the pub pack.
3. **Nobody Falls Like a Fool, Earl Thomas Conley** (was 80s, Aussie and Country). A 1985 US
   country radio hit that never charted in Australia, and it was in the Aussie pack.
4. **Let's Meet Christine Anu, The Wiggles and Christine Anu** (was Aussie and 2000s). A
   children's track in a pack meant for a pub.
5. **Onnanoko Ni Naritai, Mafumafu** (was Pub Classics, Pop and 2010s). Japanese vocaloid
   pop, in the pub pack.
6. **Speed of Love, Masayoshi Takanaka** (was 80s Rock, 80s and Pop). Japanese instrumental
   jazz fusion. There is no vocal to sing and nothing to recognise.
7. **Heavy Metal Shuffle, Kick Axe** (was 80s Rock). One of three Kick Axe tracks. The band
   never charted in Australia; the pack now leans on the acts that did.
8. **It's Good For the Soul (Walter Gibbons 12 inch Mix), The Salsoul Orchestra** (was
   Disco). A collector's twelve inch mix, not a floor filler anybody names.
9. **Twinkle Twinkle Little Star, Baby Lulu** (was Soul and Motown, and 2010s). A lullaby,
   in the Motown pack, because the artist name matched Lulu.
10. **Little Lion Man, Twinkle Twinkle Little Rock Star** (was 2010s). A lullaby cover band
    playing Mumford and Sons. The card would say Little Lion Man and the room would hear a
    music box.

Also removed on the same grounds: Country Strong by Gwyneth Paltrow, Fight the Youth by
Fishbone, Blues Is a Woman by Lou Rawls and Les McCann, Body Slam by Bootsy's Rubber Band,
and every deep cut by Wishbone Ash, Uriah Heep, Kick Axe, Krokus, Accept, Saxon, W.A.S.P.,
Vixen, Britny Fox, Faster Pussycat, The Icicle Works, Doug Stone, Neal McCoy, Aaron Tippin
and Sammy Kershaw.

Four more were removed for the room rather than for recognition: WAP by Cardi B, Super
Freaky Girl by Nicki Minaj, Hits from the Bong by Cypress Hill, and the two Dr. Dre
instrumentals in the Hip-Hop pack, which have no vocal to sing. Christmas songs came out of
Pub Classics as well (Rockin' Around the Christmas Tree, I Wish It Could Be Christmas
Everyday, Driving Home for Christmas), since they only work for a few weeks a year.

## What is still red, and what I could not do about it

`python3 tools/release-check.py --local` finishes with all six song library checks green:
every playlist points at songs that exist, no song is held twice, every song has audio, no
playlist is empty, songs know what year they are, and every decade pack holds only its
decade.

`jsc venueplay/app/musical/musical-draw.test.js` now fails three of its checks:

    FAIL 2000s: at least half the night is from the top third   27 of 60
    FAIL 90s: the night leans on the well-known end   median position 100 of 252
    FAIL 90s: at least half the night is from the top third   27 of 60

This is not a curation fault and no reordering fixes it. The draw weights a song at
1 / (1 + position / HITS_ALPHA) with HITS_ALPHA set to 45 in host.html. That constant was
tuned when the packs held 500 to 1,200 songs. On a 250 song pack the same constant spreads
sixty picks well past the top third, so the check fails no matter which songs are in the
list, because the check counts positions, not songs.

I proved that rather than guessing. Reproducing the test's own seeded generator exactly, I
searched every pack length from 180 to 500 for the five packs the test uses. The first pack
alone cannot pass at any length below 319, and with the sizes that follow, the 90s pack
would have to hold 433 songs to pass. Filling a 90s pack to 433 songs means putting back
everything that was just taken out.

The fix belongs in the page, not in the data, and it is one line:

    venueplay/app/musical/host.html    var HITS_ALPHA=45;  becomes  var HITS_ALPHA=25;

I did not make that change: I was asked not to edit any page, and a change to how a night is
dealt has to be watched in a real room before it ships. With HITS_ALPHA at 25 all five packs
pass all of their checks, measured against the same seeded generator:

    alpha 25   2000s ok   80s ok   90s ok   Pub Classics ok   Aussie ok

The test reads that constant out of the page itself, so it will pick the change up with no
edit to the test.

Three other things worth saying plainly:

- Four songs I wanted could not be resolved from the AU store and are not in the library:
  Friends in Low Places and The Dance by Garth Brooks (he has famously kept his catalogue
  off these stores), The Nosebleed Section by Hilltop Hoods, and abcdefu by GAYLE. Killing
  In the Name, Wild Boys, Never Be Like You and Astronaut In The Ocean only came back as
  live takes or remixes, so they were left out rather than added badly.
- Where a song already existed in the library, the existing id was reused rather than a
  second copy added, which is why 234 songs were added but far more pack slots were filled.
- The Disco and Funk packs could not reach 180 songs on disco and funk records alone that an
  Australian crowd would name, so both were topped up with the floor fillers that sit either
  side of the genre: Motown and Philly soul in Funk, and the modern disco revival (Uptown
  Funk, Get Lucky, 24K Magic, Happy) in Disco. If you would rather those packs were smaller
  and purer, say so and they come back down.

## Putting a song back

Everything lives in venueplay/data/song-curation-2026-09-08.json. Add the song id to the
pack list where you want it, in the position you want it (the list is most known first), then
run:

    python3 tools/curate-songs.py
    python3 tools/release-check.py --local

The tool takes a backup on its first run only, so the original library is still sitting at
venueplay/data/musical-library.backup-2026-09-08-pre-curate.json.
