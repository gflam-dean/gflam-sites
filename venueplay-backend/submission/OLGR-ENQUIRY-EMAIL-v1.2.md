Dear Technical Unit,

Thank you for your reply to us of 18 August 2026, which set out that VenuePlay's random number generator needs OLGR approval for bingo, members draws and raffles, and that the bingo tickets shown on players' phones need approval as portable electronic tickets. We have decided to apply, and this email asks four questions so that our submission arrives in the shape you want it.

What VenuePlay is. VenuePlay is Queensland-built software that a club, charity or pub uses to run its own bingo, raffle or members draw on the venue's television, with players joining from their phones. The venue conducts the game, sells any tickets and supplies the prize; VenuePlay supplies the software and, for the raffle, the members draw and bingo, the random selection. We charge the venue a subscription. We handle no money from players.

What we are asking to have approved.

1. The random number generator, described in the attached dossier, which is written to clause 4.1 of your "Random number generator minimum technical requirements" v1.5. There is no proprietary generator: every random value comes from the Web Cryptography API's crypto.getRandomValues as implemented by Cloudflare Workers (V8) on our server and by the browser on the host's tablet, which we understand clause 4.9.1 contemplates. VenuePlay's own random selection is one rejection-sampling scaler, one Fisher-Yates shuffle of 1 to 90, and three selection procedures, under 150 lines in all, every line reproduced in the dossier. The bingo code that uses them (the draw, the ticket maker, the claim rule and the rules for when the host's tablet may stand in for the server) is one named and versioned module, VP-DRAW, of about 1,200 lines in two files, supplied in full with the SHA-256 of each file; our live server reports the module's version and manifest hash at a public health address so you can check what is running against what is described. The intended uses are 90-ball bingo (1 to 90 without replacement, one ball per host tap), raffles (winning ticket numbers over a venue-typed range, 1 to 50 winners) and members draws (one member from a members list), conducted by eligible associations and, for free-entry promotional games, by licensed venues in Queensland, for category 1, 2 and 4 games with prizes up to AUD 20,000. Category 3 games are outside the intended use.

2. The bingo ticket displayed on a player's phone. The ticket is generated by our server from the same generator, allotted to one identified phone, and held as a master copy by our server, never by the phone or by the host's tablet. When a player claims, our server checks its own copy of the ticket against its own record of the numbers called, records the verdict, and the host may confirm only a claim the server has judged a win. Each phone signs its join and its claim with a key only it holds, which the host's console verifies before carrying the claim to the server. The dossier covers the generator and the ticket software together.

Our four questions.

(a) Lodgement. We have read your Submission Requirements v1.7. Is a submission letter addressed to the Executive Director, OLGR, ATTN: Technical Unit, with the Certification and Indemnity form, sent to this address, the complete application under s 98, or is there an approved form or a portal we should use? And should the RNG and the phone ticket be one submission or two?

(b) Fees. We understand the evaluation fee is hourly under Schedule 2 of the Charitable and Non-Profit Gaming Regulation and is payable if OLGR carries out the evaluation. Can you give us an indication of the hours a submission of this size usually takes, so we can budget?

(c) Evaluator. Under s 99 you may evaluate the equipment yourselves or direct us to an approved evaluator. Given the size of this submission (a known commercial entropy source, one scaler, one shuffle, three selection procedures and one bingo module), we would prefer the Unit to evaluate it if you are able to. Would you expect to evaluate it within the Unit, or direct us to BMM, ENEX, GTA or QALAB? If the latter, we will follow section 5.2 of the Submission Requirements and make a concept submission to you first; please tell us what you would like in it, and whether you have a preference among the four for this category.

(d) The phone ticket. Which published requirements will the ticket software be assessed against, beyond the RNG requirements and the Submission Requirements' software sections? In particular, does the Gaming terminal technical standard apply to a player's own phone, and what do you want to see on ticket generation, allotment, protection from alteration, claim verification and records?

Two further points you may want to know now. First, our software is updated often; we would like to agree with you that the named module VP-DRAW, identified by its manifest hash and listed file by file in the submission, is the approved equipment, so that s 100B(2) is engaged only when one of those files changes. Second, if our server does not answer within four seconds, the bingo console on the host's tablet keeps calling that game from the remaining pool, one ball at a time, and reports each ball to the server, which accepts it only against its own record of having failed; the same applies to the opening deal of tickets before the first ball. The design reason and the record it leaves are in sections 5.3 and 5.5 of the dossier, and the module carries a one-line switch that turns all of it off for Queensland venues if you would prefer that. We would rather you see it now than find it later.

We are in Oxenford and can come to George Street for a presentation if that is useful, or take a call most afternoons. I am the person responsible for the submission and the contact for technical enquiries.
