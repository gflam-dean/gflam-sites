Questions

Can I put it on more than one screen?
As many as you like, at no extra cost. Open the same screen link on each one and they all show the same thing at the same moment. Worth doing if you have a bistro that cannot see the main bar's telly, or a beer garden with its own screen: people stay in the game instead of drifting off.
When can I start?
Is tonight too soon? You can sign up and start straight away.
How much does it cost?
$3 per player a month, based on your venue's max players per session. Pay monthly, or save with annual at $2.85 a player. No setup fees, no packs to buy, no lock-in.
What about regulars who won't use a phone?
They can still play trivia and musical bingo on paper. Print up to ten trivia answer sheets or musical bingo cards a night from the host console, before the night starts. Trivia teams on paper hand their sheet to the host at the end of each round, before the answers go up on the screen, and the host adds their score to the leaderboard. A paper musical bingo card is checked by typing its number into the console. Paper players count towards your players and your bill the same as phones do. Moving a trivia crowd over from paper? In your free month you can print a trivia sheet for every player on your plan, so your regulars can switch across at their own pace. VenuePlay does not print bingo tickets. For bingo, keep using the paper books you already buy, and the host checks those wins against the board.
Do players need to download an app?
No. They scan a code and play straight from their phone's browser. No app, no login, no fuss, which means more of the room actually joins in.
What kind of venues is it for?
Pubs, clubs, bowls clubs and RSLs: anyone running a regular entertainment night, or wanting to start one. If you've got a screen and a crowd, you're set.
How does billing work?
A simple subscription priced per player, per month, based on your venue's max players per session. Pay monthly at $3 a player, or yearly for $2.85 a player. New venues get a free first month, then billing recurs at your rate. Cancel any time before your first payment and you are never charged. If a night runs over your max, each extra player that night is $2, and your host has to tap OK on the screen before anything is added, so a busy night can never bill you by surprise.
What if more players turn up than my plan?
Nothing happens without your host. Set your max to your usual room, and the moment a night runs over it your host sees what it costs and taps OK before the game starts, so you are never billed for a night nobody agreed to. Extra players are $2 each for that night. Go over three nights running and that is your crowd, not a big night: the third one is $1 a head and we lift your max to the number you hit all three times, from your next bill. It works out cheaper than paying per head every week. You can drop back any time from your account, and we always email before your bill changes.
Can I change my max players?
Any time, from your account. Adding players applies straight away so you are covered that night, and the extra is on your next bill. Dropping down takes effect at your next renewal, so you keep what you have paid for until then. On an annual plan, added players are charged pro rata to your renewal date, and if you drop your max the unused value is not lost: it is held as a credit against your next renewal.
What do I need to run it?
A screen, an internet connection, and a way to get the display onto that screen: a smart TV with a browser, a streaming stick, or an HDMI cable from a laptop or tablet all work. Players bring their own phones. For a rock-solid night we'd point you toward a stick or a cable over wireless casting, since that doesn't lean on venue wifi.
Is there a lock-in contract?
No lock-in. It's a rolling subscription you can cancel anytime. Your rate stays the same for as long as you're with us, and if you cancel before your first payment, you're never charged.
What support do I get?
We get your first night running with you, and after that we are on the end of an email or the phone whenever something comes up: the number is on our Help page, and if a game is on right now, press 2. The whole thing is built so your bar staff can run it without a tech in the room.
Can players win prizes?
Yep, you set the prizes, exactly like you do now. Bar tabs, meat trays, members jackpots, vouchers, whatever suits your venue. Your venue conducts the game and VenuePlay supplies the software: your host runs it on the night, confirms each winner, and winners claim their prize from the host. The prizes are yours to decide.
Is players' data safe?
Players join with just a name, no account, no login, nothing they have to hand over. Anything captured is opt-in and handled under Australian privacy rules.
I am a trivia host at multiple venues. Do I keep the players' details?
No, and that's by design. A venue's player data belongs to that venue. If you run games at a venue you don't own, VenuePlay only ever collects a player's first and last name for the game itself: it can't switch on email, mobile, postcode or marketing opt-in, so there's no marketing list to take. Nothing is held on the side or handed over later. Each venue's opt-in players stay with that venue.
Who's behind it?
VenuePlay is built by Gflam Touring, who run bingo, live music and entertainment across Australia. It's the same show experience, built into a platform any venue can run.
Do the gaming rules change from state to state?
They do, and it is worth knowing before you plan a night. Trivia is a game of skill, so it sits outside gaming rules everywhere in Australia and any venue can run it. Musical bingo is a music game, not bingo: players mark off songs, so the bingo rules do not apply to it. Bingo, raffles and members draws are gaming, and every state treats them a little differently, mostly around who can charge for entry and who keeps the proceeds. Broadly: clubs and not-for-profits have the most room to run a paid game, and pubs are usually limited to free entry. We will tell you what applies to your venue when you set up, and point you at your own state's regulator. You conduct the game and provide the prizes; we supply the software.
Can players use their phones for bingo tickets in my state?
In most of the country, yes, ACT included, and it is the whole point of the product. Three states still expect a printed bingo ticket in the player's hand: South Australia, where the rules recognise only physical bingo sheets bought from a licensed supplier; Victoria, where the law does not allow bingo tickets on a player's phone; and Tasmania, where the bingo rules are written around a printed card that the supervisor collects to verify a win. In those three, your host still runs the night on VenuePlay: the calling, the board on the big screen, the pattern and the prizes all work exactly the same, and the called numbers stay up so a shout can be checked against the screen. Your players mark off paper tickets the way they always have. What you lose is the phone side: no digital tickets, and no automatic check of a winning ticket, so a claim is verified by eye against the board as it always was. Musical bingo is not bingo, so its phone cards work in every state.
We are a pub in South Australia or the Northern Territory. Can we run bingo?
Not number bingo, and that comes from the law rather than from us. In South Australia only a not-for-profit organisation may run bingo, and in the Northern Territory only an approved association, so pubs and hotels there will not see number bingo in VenuePlay. Musical bingo is a music game, not bingo, so it is yours, along with trivia and members draws, and the price is the same. If a club or charity runs the bingo at your venue, tick the box when you sign up or email hello@venueplay.com.au, and we will switch it on once we have seen who runs it.
What else is different in South Australia and the Northern Territory?
A few things around members draws and raffles, and your host is shown your state's rules before each night. In South Australia a pub's members draw is a trade promotion: up to $5,000 in prizes, jackpot included, and the member drawn wins whether or not they are in the room, so at a pub the jackpot never rolls over because the winner is not there. Only not-for-profits may sell raffle tickets there, so a pub's raffle is free entry. In the Northern Territory a prize cannot be only alcohol, nobody under 18 can win, staff and their families cannot enter a pub's members draw, and the winners are named in a newspaper. Trivia is a game of skill in both, and VenuePlay never breaks a trivia tie at random.
Do we need approval from the gaming regulator to use VenuePlay?
For trivia, no, anywhere. Musical bingo is a music game, not bingo, so the bingo approvals do not apply to it. For bingo, raffles and members draws, it depends on your state. Queensland asks for the random number generator to be approved by OLGR before it draws bingo, members draws or raffles, and we are working through that submission. Most other states have no approval step at all and simply require the draw to be fair and random, which ours is: it uses the same cryptographic method banks and browsers use, and we have the statistical test results to show it. If your state needs anything from us, we will tell you rather than leave you to find out.
