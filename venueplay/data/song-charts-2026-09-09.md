# Songs the charts say we were missing

9 September 2026. Written after the change was applied and the gate was run.

## What Dean asked

"Are we able to have an agent look at charts etc to see if there are any songs
missing from our list and then add them?", with the rule set the day before:
"the bangers that we would have heard in Australia, not something we haven't
heard of".

So this is not a bulk load. Nothing went in on a hunch. Every song added here
was bought, voted for or taken to number one by Australians, and every one of
them has a thirty second preview clip that will actually play on the TV.

## Where the songs came from

Every page below was read as raw Wikipedia source, not scraped from a screen,
so the numbers can be checked line by line.

- Kent Music Report and ARIA year-end singles charts, 55 pages covering 1970 to 2024.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_top_25_singles_for_1975_in_Australia is
  the shape of them. 1970 to 2018 are top 25 pages. 2019 to 2024 have no top 25
  page, so the "top ten singles" page for each of those years was used instead,
  which carries both the peak position and the year-end position.
- Australian number-one singles, 29 pages.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_number-one_singles_in_Australia_during_the_1970s
  for the 1960s to the 1990s, then one page a year from 2000, for example
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_number-one_singles_of_2015_(Australia)
- triple j Hottest 100 of All Time, 2009:
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_J%27s_Hottest_100_of_All_Time,_2009
- triple j Hottest 100 of All Time, 1998:
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_J%27s_Hottest_100_of_All_Time,_1998
- triple j Hottest 100 of the Past 20 Years:
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_J%27s_Hottest_100_of_the_Past_20_Years
- triple j Hottest 100 of Australian Songs:
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_J%27s_Hottest_100_of_Australian_Songs
- Preview clips, cover art and release years: the iTunes AU search endpoint,
  https://itunes.apple.com/search?country=AU&media=music&entity=song&term=...

### The one source that was asked for and does not exist

The brief asked for the triple j Hottest 100 of All Time lists from 2009 and
2013. There was no all time countdown in 2013. What triple j ran that year was
the Hottest 100 of the Past 20 Years, so that is the list used, and the 1998 all
time countdown was added as well to cover the same ground. This is stated rather
than papered over: no chart was invented to fill the gap.

The iTunes store also blocks a caller that asks too fast. The first run was cut
off by it, twice. The lookups were re-run at one every five seconds, and a
block is now waited out rather than recorded as a miss, because a miss reads
exactly like a song with no preview clip and would have quietly shrunk the result.

## What was deliberately left out

- 71 songs that reached number one or the top ten here but that no pub would
  name today. The rule: a song with no year-end top 25 finish and no all time
  countdown placing only stays if it charted from 1970 on, or if the library
  already holds three or more songs by that act. 55 Days at Peking by Rob E.G.
  was a number one in 1963. A Scottish Soldier by Andy Stewart was a number one.
  Neither is a musical bingo square.
- 87 songs with no usable match: no preview clip in the Australian store, only a
  karaoke or tribute recording, or a title the library already holds under a
  slightly different name.
- 7 Christmas songs and one children's song. Bob the Builder went to number
  one here in 2001 and would still empty the room.

### Four songs went in as the wrong recording

The Australian store had no plain studio version with a preview clip for these,
so an alternate take went in rather than losing the song. The tune is right and
the title on the card is right; the recording is not the one that charted:

- Sexy Bitch, David Guetta, the extended version
- Talk Dirty, Jason Derulo, the acoustic version
- The Newcastle Song, Bob Hudson, a live recording
- Old Time Rock and Roll, Bob Seger, a live recording

Say the word and any of the four comes straight back out.

- Anything below a year-end top 25 finish that was not also a number one, a top
  ten peak or a triple j all time placing. That is where the line was drawn and
  it is the reason the list is this size rather than several thousand.

## How to re-run it

    python3 tools/add-charted-songs.py
    python3 tools/release-check.py --local

The tool is add only. It never reorders a pack, never removes a song and never
rewrites a pack list. Running it twice changes nothing the second time.
